Reference
EDS Guide
Stability Before Strength — Always
With hypermobile EDS, joint laxity means ligaments cannot reliably hold joints in place. Muscle strength and neuromuscular control must compensate. Master slow, controlled bodyweight versions before adding load. A sloppy rep at 5 lbs is more dangerous than a heavy lift with perfect form.
Why Rowing is Ideal for EDS
Rowing is low-impact and bilaterally symmetrical. It builds scapular stabilizers, grip endurance, and posterior chain simultaneously — directly addressing EDS weak points. Don't force the catch past comfortable knee compression. Drive powerfully but avoid hyperextending the knee at the finish.
Anterior Shin Splints in EDS
Tibialis anterior is overworking to compensate for ankle instability — doing two jobs at once. Strengthen both it AND the ankle stabilizers. Tibialis raises and peroneal work are your primary tools. Allow 4–6 weeks. Do not push through acute shin pain.
Wrist Progression Protocol
  • Start non-weight-bearing: circles, finger extensions, towel grip
  • Wall push-ups before floor push-ups
  • Fist push-ups on floor to keep wrist neutral if needed
  • Forearm plank only until wrist strength is established
  • No plank-ups yet — wrist transition under load is high-risk
  • Rowing is excellent for grip and wrist stability at moderate intensity
Red Flag Stop Signs
  • Clunking, clicking, or giving-way in any joint
  • Sharp or burning joint pain (distinct from muscle burn)
  • Joint visibly or palpably shifting out of position
  • Numbness or tingling distal to a joint
  • Disproportionate pain after a session vs. normal DOMS
RPE Scale
Stay RPE 6–7 for working sets. Joint protection matters more than training close to failure.
